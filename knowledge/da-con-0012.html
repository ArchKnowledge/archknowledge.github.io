Semantic Model

An explicit, governed representation of meaning — concepts, their definitions, identity criteria and semantic relationships such as specialization, generalization, part-whole and equivalence.

Draft knowledge. Status proposed, quality draft. Not yet reviewed or approved by a human architect.

Definition

A Semantic Model is an explicit, governed representation of meaning: concepts, their definitions and identity criteria, and semantic relationships such as specialization, generalization, part-whole and equivalence.

Explanation

The semantic model answers "what does something mean?" — deliberately separated from "how is that meaning represented in an information model?" (Information Model) and "how is it technically implemented?" (physical and technical layers).

Semantic models range in formality:

Form Formality Typical standard
Controlled Vocabulary list of approved terms —
Taxonomy hierarchy (broader/narrower) SKOS
Thesaurus hierarchy + associative relations + synonyms SKOS
Ontology classes, properties, axioms, reasoning OWL 2 on RDF 1.1

Choosing the formality is supported by Semantic Modeling Approach Decision Tree.

Distinctions

  • Versus Conceptual Data Model: the conceptual model scopes data; the semantic model defines meaning, including concepts that never become data.
  • Semantic relationships (specializes, equivalent-to) are different from data relationships (foreign keys).

Examples

  • "Natural Person" specializes "Party"; "Client" is equivalent to "Customer" in the sales vocabulary.
