Data Lifecycle

The sequence of stages data passes through — creation or acquisition, storage, use, change, sharing, archiving and disposal — together with the rules governing each transition.

Draft knowledge. Status proposed, quality draft. Not yet reviewed or approved by a human architect.

Definition

The Data Lifecycle is the sequence of stages data passes through — creation or acquisition, storage, use, change, sharing, archiving and disposal — together with the rules governing each transition.

Explanation

Architecturally relevant lifecycle decisions:

  • Change: is history kept, and which kind? → Temporal Data Management.
  • Retention: how long must data be kept (legal, business), and how long may it be kept (privacy: storage limitation under GDPR)?
  • Archiving: when does data move to cheaper, less accessible storage, and can it still be queried?
  • Disposal: deletion, anonymisation or pseudonymisation; proving it happened.

Retention obligations and deletion obligations can conflict (e.g. audit history vs. right to erasure). Such conflicts must be flagged and decided by the Data Owner with the Privacy Specialist, not silently resolved in design.

Distinctions

  • Data lifecycle vs knowledge lifecycle: the Governance Lifecycle governs knowledge objects and architecture artifacts, not business data.

Examples

  • Claims data: active use 2 years, archive 5 years, anonymise afterwards unless under litigation hold.
