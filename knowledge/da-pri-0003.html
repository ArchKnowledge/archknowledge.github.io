Principle · Architecture

Decisions Belong to Their Layer

Each architecture decision is taken at the layer where it belongs, by the role with decision rights for that layer, and lower layers do not silently override higher-layer decisions.

Draft knowledge. Status proposed, quality draft. Not yet reviewed or approved by a human architect.

Statement

Each architecture decision shall be taken at the layer where it belongs, by the role with decision rights for that layer; lower layers shall not silently override decisions of higher layers.

Rationale

Mixing layers produces technology lock-in of meaning (e.g. semantics defined by a database product) and untraceable decisions.

Implications

  • Decision records state their layer (layer metadata).
  • A physical design that cannot honour an information-level requirement is escalated, not worked around (Architecture Change Process Architect).

Consequences

  • Positive: clean traceability, replaceable technology.
  • Negative: more explicit hand-offs between roles.

Applicability

All architecture work covered by this knowledge base.

Exceptions

Small solutions where one person holds all roles still record the layer, but may combine approval steps.
