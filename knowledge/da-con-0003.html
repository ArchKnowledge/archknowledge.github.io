Concept · Foundations

Architecture Layer

A level of abstraction in the architecture stack (business, conceptual, semantic, information, logical, physical, technical) with its own questions, artifacts and decision rights.

Draft knowledge. Status proposed, quality draft. Not yet reviewed or approved by a human architect.

Definition

An Architecture Layer is a level of abstraction in the architecture stack with its own questions, artifacts and decision rights. This knowledge base distinguishes seven layers (machine-readable in 00-system/taxonomy/layers.yaml):

# Layer Central question Typical decisions
1 Business What does the organisation do and need? scope, business rules, accountability
2 Conceptual Which things and relationships matter? concept scope, high-level relationships
3 Semantic What exactly does each concept mean? definitions, identity, specialization, equivalence
4 Information How is meaning represented as information structures, paradigm-independent? classes, attributes, cardinalities, information-level history
5 Logical Data How is information structured in a chosen paradigm? normalization, keys, temporal modeling approach
6 Physical Data How is the logical model realized in a store? data types, partitioning, indexing, physical history mechanism
7 Technical Implementation Which technologies and operations implement it? products, pipelines, deployment

Explanation

Layers are not interchangeable. A decision taken at the wrong layer produces lock-in or ambiguity: e.g. defining the meaning of "customer" in a physical table comment (Implicit Semantics) or choosing a history mechanism before stating the historical requirement (Historical Requirements Are Explicit).

The chain of realization is:

Business Meaning → Governed Concept → Semantic Model → Information Model → Logical Data Model → Physical Data Model → Technical Implementation

Each step realizes the step above and is governed by the corresponding level of the Decision Rights Model Architect.

Distinctions

  • A layer is not a system tier (presentation / application / data). System tiers are a technical decomposition that lives entirely within the technical layer.
  • The separation of semantic from information layer is an interpretation adopted by this knowledge base: the semantic layer answers "what does it mean?", the information layer answers "how is that meaning represented in an information model?".

Examples

  • "A customer is a party that has at least one active agreement" → semantic layer.
  • "Customer has attribute customer since with cardinality 1..1" → information layer.
  • "customer_since DATE NOT NULL partitioned by year" → physical layer.

Open Questions

  • Many frameworks merge conceptual and semantic, or information and logical. The seven-layer separation needs review by the domain owner for Foundations.
