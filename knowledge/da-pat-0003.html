Pattern · Analytics & BI

Slowly Changing Dimension Type 2

In a dimensional model, preserve history of dimension attributes by inserting a new dimension row with its own surrogate key and effective period for each change.

Draft knowledge. Status proposed, quality draft. Not yet reviewed or approved by a human architect.

Problem

Analytical facts must be reported against the dimension attributes that were valid when the fact occurred (e.g. customer segment at sale time).

Context

Dimensional data warehouses and marts.

Forces

  • Reports need both "as was" and sometimes "as is" views.
  • Fact tables should join simply to dimensions.

Solution

On change of a tracked attribute, expire the current dimension row (effective_to, is_current = false) and insert a new row with a new surrogate key. Facts reference the surrogate key valid at the fact's time.

Architecture

dim_customer(sk, customer_bk, segment, effective_from, effective_to, is_current)
fact_sales(customer_sk → dim_customer.sk, date_sk, amount)

Advantages

  • Simple, well-known, tool-supported.
  • Fast "as was" reporting.

Disadvantages

  • Typically single time dimension; backdated corrections are awkward.
  • Dimension growth with volatile attributes.

Risks

  • Tracking too many attributes explodes dimension size.
  • Late-arriving facts can bind to the wrong version.

When to Use

  • Analytical history of descriptive attributes.

When Not to Use

  • Operational reconstruction or legal reproducibility → Bitemporal Data Model.

Alternatives

SCD Type 1 (overwrite), SCD Type 4 (history table), Bitemporal Data Model.

Examples

  • Sales by customer segment as the segment was at sale time.

  • Select Historical Data Strategy Professional
