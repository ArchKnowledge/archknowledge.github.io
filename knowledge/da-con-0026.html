Concept · Master Data

Golden Record

The consolidated, best-known version of a master data entity, assembled from one or more source records according to survivorship rules.

Draft knowledge. Status proposed, quality draft. Not yet reviewed or approved by a human architect.

Definition

A Golden Record is the consolidated, best-known version of a master data entity, assembled from one or more source records according to matching and survivorship rules.

Explanation

Creating a golden record requires: identity resolution (match), linking source records (cross-reference), survivorship rules per attribute (which source wins, recency, completeness), and lineage back to contributing records. It is best-known, not true: its quality depends on sources and rules.

Distinctions

  • A golden record is not necessarily physically stored: in a registry-style hub it can be virtual.

Examples

  • One customer record combining the address from the CRM (most recent) and the birth date from the policy system (most trusted).
