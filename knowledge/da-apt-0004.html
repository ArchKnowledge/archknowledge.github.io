Anti-pattern · Architecture Governance

Governance as Afterthought

Governance is applied as a documentation and approval step at the end of delivery, instead of being embedded in the architecture and workflow.

Draft knowledge. Status proposed, quality draft. Not yet reviewed or approved by a human architect.

Problem

Architecture review and data governance happen after design is finished, as a gate to "get the stamp".

Symptoms

  • Review boards reject or wave through finished work.
  • Documentation written after go-live.
  • Governance perceived as bureaucracy.

Root Causes

  • Governance modeled as documents rather than decisions; no decision rights per layer.

Consequences

  • Late, expensive rework or accepted non-compliance.

Detection

  • Compare decision dates to implementation dates; count ADRs created after deployment.

Remediation

  • Define decision rights per layer (Decision Rights Model Architect); embed controls in the workflow (Architecture Change Process Architect); automate evidence.

Alternative Patterns

Architecture Change Process Architect, Data Contract.

When It May Be Acceptable

Never as a default; post-hoc review is acceptable for emergency changes, with retroactive recording.
