Decision · Architecture Governance

Separate Lifecycle Status from Knowledge Quality

Track governance lifecycle (`status`) and knowledge quality (`quality`) as two separate fields, reconcile the spec's Draft/Proposed ambiguity with two initial states, and treat the spec's `approval_status` as derived from `status`.

Draft knowledge. Status proposed, quality draft. Not yet reviewed or approved by a human architect.

Context

The spec defines a governance lifecycle (§13: Proposed … Retired), a quality status (§17: Draft … Superseded), and an approval_status field (§16). The §13 transition example starts at "Draft → Review" while the lifecycle list starts at "Proposed".

Problem

How to represent these without redundant, contradictory fields?

Options

  1. One combined status field.
  2. Two fields: status (governance lifecycle) and quality (content trust); approval_status derived.
  3. Three fields as listed in the spec.

Evaluation Criteria

Non-redundancy, expressiveness, validator enforceability, fidelity to spec.

Trade-offs

  • Option 1 loses information (a published object can still be of limited quality).
  • Option 3 creates contradictions (status: draft with approval_status: approved).

Decision

Proposed: option 2. - status: draft → proposed → under-review → validated → approved → published → (implemented) → deprecated → retired, plus rejected. Both draft and proposed are initial states; the LLM may set at most proposed. - quality: draft | reviewed | validated | authoritative | deprecated | superseded, with constraints on the compatible status. - approval_status is not stored; it is derived (approved: true for approved/published/implemented/deprecated in 00-system/governance/lifecycle.yaml).

Flagged deviation from spec §16 (which lists approval_status as a field) — requires human approval.

Consequences

  • Validator enforces status–quality compatibility and allowed transitions (with --base).

Assumptions

  • Reviewers accept the derived approval status.

Constraints

  • Git history is the audit log of transitions.
