Concept · Data Governance

Data Governance

The exercise of authority, control and shared decision-making over the management of data assets — who may decide what about data, on which basis, and how compliance is ensured.

Draft knowledge. Status proposed, quality draft. Not yet reviewed or approved by a human architect.

Definition

Data Governance is the exercise of authority, control and shared decision-making over the management of data assets: who may decide what about data, on which basis, and how compliance is ensured.

Explanation

Data governance answers the questions of spec §10:

  • Who can decide? What can they decide? At which layer?
  • Based on which standards? What evidence is required?
  • Who approves? What is generated after approval?
  • How is the decision recorded and how can it be changed?

These questions are answered structurally in Decision Rights Model Architect, Governance Roles, Governance Lifecycle and the Architecture Governance Model. Governance is a first-class architectural layer, not a document produced at the end (Governance Is Embedded in Delivery; anti-pattern: Governance as Afterthought).

Distinctions

  • Governance vs management: governance decides and oversees; data management executes.
  • Data governance vs architecture governance: data governance is about data assets; architecture governance is about architecture decisions and artifacts. They share roles and decision rights and are modeled together in Architecture Governance Model.

Examples

  • A data owner approves the definition of "Active Customer"; a steward maintains the related quality rules.
