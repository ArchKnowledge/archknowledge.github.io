Concept · Data Modeling

Temporal Data Management

The capture, storage and querying of how data and knowledge about data change over time, including valid time (when a fact is true in reality) and transaction time (when the fact was recorded).

Draft knowledge. Status proposed, quality draft. Not yet reviewed or approved by a human architect.

Definition

Temporal Data Management is the capture, storage and querying of how data — and knowledge about data — changes over time.

Explanation

Two time dimensions are commonly distinguished:

  • Valid time (application time, business time): when a fact is true in the modeled reality.
  • Transaction time (system time): when the fact was recorded in, and removed from, the database.

A model supporting both is bitemporal. The concept is technology-neutral; it is realized by patterns such as Bitemporal Data Model, System-Versioned History Table, Slowly Changing Dimension Type 2 and Event Sourcing, and implemented by technologies such as SQL Server Temporal Tables.

The requirement must be stated before the mechanism is chosen (Historical Requirements Are Explicit). The choice is supported by Database History Pattern Decision Tree.

Distinctions

  • History versus audit: an audit trail records who changed what and when for accountability; history supports reconstruction and temporal querying. They overlap but are not identical.
  • History versus backup: backups restore a whole state; they are not a queryable history.

Examples

  • "What was the customer's address on 1 March, as we knew it on 15 March?" requires both valid and transaction time.
