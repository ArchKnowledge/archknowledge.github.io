Principle · Semantic Architecture

Meaning Before Representation

The meaning of a concept is defined and governed before, and independently of, its representation in information and data models.

Draft knowledge. Status proposed, quality draft. Not yet reviewed or approved by a human architect.

Statement

The meaning of a concept shall be defined and governed before, and independently of, its representation in information and data models.

Rationale

When meaning is only implicit in models, code or column names, every consumer reinterprets it. Integration, migration and analytics then fail on semantic mismatch rather than on technology.

Implications

  • A governed Business Concept exists (at least proposed) before an entity representing it is approved.
  • Model elements link to concepts (Concept-to-Model Traceability).
  • Changing a definition is a semantic governance decision, not a model change (Decision Rights Model Architect).

Consequences

  • Positive: stable meaning across systems; cheaper integration and migration.
  • Negative: upfront effort; risk of analysis paralysis if applied dogmatically to low-value data.

Applicability

Shared, integrated and regulated data; master and reference data.

Exceptions

Local, single-application data without sharing ambitions. Prototypes, provided concepts are backfilled before production.
