Ontology

A formal, explicit specification of concepts in a domain, their properties and relationships, and logical axioms, expressed so that machines can reason over it.

Draft knowledge. Status proposed, quality draft. Not yet reviewed or approved by a human architect.

Definition

An Ontology is a formal, explicit specification of the concepts in a domain, their properties and relationships, and logical axioms (e.g. disjointness, cardinality, equivalence), expressed so that machines can reason over it.

Explanation

Ontologies are the most formal form of Semantic Model. On the web-standards stack they are typically expressed in OWL 2 serialized as RDF 1.1. Their value lies in inference (deriving implicit facts), consistency checking and integration of heterogeneous sources through shared meaning.

Distinctions

  • Versus Taxonomy: a taxonomy only has broader/narrower; an ontology has typed properties and axioms.
  • Versus Information Model: an ontology describes meaning under an open-world assumption; an information model describes the structure of records, typically under a closed-world assumption. Confusing the two leads to surprising validation behaviour.

Examples

  • A domain ontology stating that every Vehicle Registration relates to exactly one Vehicle and that Natural Person and Legal Person are disjoint.
