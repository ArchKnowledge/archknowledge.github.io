About

Why a knowledge graph for architecture?

Most architecture knowledge exists. It is just not connected — so it is re-created, re-debated and lost.

The problem: fragmentation

In most organisations, architecture knowledge lives in:

  • Documents — architecture descriptions that are outdated the day they are approved.
  • Diagrams — pictures of structures, without the reasoning behind them.
  • Spreadsheets — inventories and RACI matrices disconnected from the models they govern.
  • Standards — external and internal, rarely linked to the decisions they constrain.
  • Project repositories — decisions buried in project folders that close with the project.
  • Individual experts — knowledge that leaves when people do.

The consequence

The same questions are answered again in every project: which history pattern fits, who decides on a definition, which standard applies, what went wrong last time. Answers are inconsistent, and decisions cannot be traced to the principles and knowledge they rest on.

The Architecture Knowledge Graph treats each piece of knowledge as an object with an identity, metadata, a lifecycle and explicit relationships to other objects. Documents become views of the graph instead of its containers.

What makes it different

Objects, not documents

Every concept, pattern or decision is one object with an immutable ID, a type and required metadata.

Relationships are knowledge

57 controlled relationship types — realizes, governed-by, constrained-by — instead of vague “see also” links.

Layers kept apart

Meaning, information, logical and physical design, and technology are separate layers with their own decisions and decision rights.

Governed

Knowledge moves through a lifecycle from draft to approved, checked by automated rules and approved by people. Governance

Honest about uncertainty

Each object says whether it is a fact, a standard, common practice, interpretation or recommendation, and how confident it is. Sources are never invented.

Portable

Markdown, YAML and Git as the source; JSON, CSV and RDF as exports. No lock-in to this website or any tool.

Who it is for

  • Data architects and information architects
  • Enterprise and solution architects
  • Semantic architects and ontology engineers
  • Data governance professionals, data owners and stewards
  • Architecture teams and CDO / CTO organisations

What it is not

  • Not a vendor's product documentation: technologies are described as realisations of neutral concepts.
  • Not an AI that decides for you: AI helps draft and link knowledge; people review and approve it.
  • Not finished: it is an early, openly labelled draft.
